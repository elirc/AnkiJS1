Good ideas deserve credit.

Recall combines original engineering scenarios with openly licensed implementation exercises. The questions and answers are included in your offline library.

Original Recall curriculum

880 original cards: 72 reviewed scenarios, 112 core questions, and 144 engineering scenarios covering SQL, distributed systems, APIs, security, TypeScript, testing, observability, processes, and deployment, plus 160 beginner cards teaching 80 ideas through original explanations, analogies, examples, mistakes, and practice questions, plus 200 follow-on cards teaching 100 additional ideas in the Keep going collection. Another 192 original cards teach 96 C# and .NET web development lessons. These cover C#, LINQ, async, ASP.NET Core, EF Core, security, web UIs, testing, deployment, and a notes-app project path, using .NET 10 as the baseline. The Keep going lessons cover TypeScript, Python, debugging, testing, accessible interfaces, HTTP APIs, databases, security, delivery, and real CRUD app problems. Decks link to official documentation for further study; the lesson prose and examples are original Recall material.

30 seconds of code

1,991 cards are adapted from 30 seconds of code, by its authors and contributors, under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International. These adapted cards retain that license. Each answer links to its pinned source file.

Source revision: f1b2d5a65c32432a877de94a749be40a396ebed2.

Adaptations include splitting articles into smaller question-and-answer units, adding recall prompts and code-completion exercises, removing embedded media and navigation, normalizing links and formatting, and correcting the declaration in an asynchronous loop example. The quality review also rewrites 14 examples for self-contained context, safe text rendering, keyboard behavior, state semantics, and explicit edge cases. Source-site code-tab wrappers are removed from portable Markdown. The answer reader also offers related beginner lessons and jargon definitions while preserving the full original answer and attribution. Sections requiring missing context, some obsolete APIs, and selected problematic examples were excluded, along with DSA, coding puzzles, and unrelated mathematical exercises. Attribution does not imply endorsement of Recall.

The retained adapted base collection contains 1,211 explanation cards and 780 code-completion cards. Related exercises are separated in the daily new-card queue.

Retired source library

Earlier versions also bundled a much larger generated collection built from pinned documentation archives. Version 15 retires it: the collection was too large to install and study on a phone in short sessions, and its quality was uneven, because selection was automatic and semantic review only ever covered a sample of it. Its cards and its bundled license notices are no longer shipped, and an upgrade removes the retired cards you never edited while keeping any you rewrote. The 48 engineering missions are original assignments and remain.

The library that ships now is 2,871 cards: the 880 original Recall cards and the 1,991 adapted cards described above. Every card passes structural checks for identity, duplicates, code fences, and credits. Authored cards were reviewed by hand; the adapted examples were not all executed.

How this library is maintained

The project includes the source revision, full license, build scripts, and checks for duplicate questions, stable unique identities, complete answers, and Markdown formatting. Original scenarios are authored separately from adapted source material. You can edit or suspend any card to fit your work and learning goals.
